Overview

TL;DR

The problem

Students struggle to find open research roles. Professors get flooded with cold emails that are hard to sort and answer.

What I did

I designed a two-sided product experience that matches student interest with lab openings and gives professors a clearer intake path.

The outcome

[OUTCOME: what shipped and who used it]

Context

Context and challenge

Undergraduate and early graduate students want research experience, but openings are scattered across department pages, personal sites, and word of mouth. Professors want capable students, yet their inboxes are not built for recruiting.

The product had to serve both sides without making either group do work that felt unfair.

One system should reduce noise for professors while making research opportunities findable for students.

Goals

Goals

  1. Help students discover labs that fit their skills and interests without endless cold outreach.
  2. Give professors a structured way to post openings and review interested students.
  3. Keep the handoff between design and engineering clear enough to ship in one semester.

Decisions

Design decisions

Two-sided matching without a noisy feed

The decision

I designed distinct student and professor surfaces that meet at openings, interest signals, and review, instead of one shared social feed.

Why

Students need discovery. Professors need triage. A single feed would optimize for neither.

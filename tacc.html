Musical Molecules

An interactive exhibit that turns infrared fingerprints into something visitors can see, tap, and hear.

Final product

Prototype screens from the Chemistry Themed Animation build: molecule picker, timed intro, and bond-frequency explorer.

Choose a molecule, watch a short intro, then tap spectrum dips to see and hear each bond.

TL;DR

The problem

Infrared spectra and molecular vibration are abstract on paper. Public visitors rarely get a way to connect a dip on a graph to a bond that moves.

What I did

I designed and prototyped Musical Molecules on the front end: five familiar compounds, short IR intros, and a Hear My Frequency mode that maps spectrum dips to bond motion and tone. Backend architecture is in planning.

The outcome

A working React exhibit prototype for Vanillin and four companion molecules, built for TACC public-facing molecular dynamics storytelling. Backend work has not started in earnest yet. Still iterating with educators and science partners.

Context and challenge

At TACC I am shaping interactive UI for a molecular dynamics exhibit. The science is real IR fingerprints: each molecule absorbs light at specific frequencies, and those dips identify bonds.

The design problem is translation. Graph axes and wavenumbers do not teach a museum visitor. Everyday molecules do: vanilla, aspirin, coffee, nail polish remover, corn ethanol. The exhibit has to make the fingerprint feel tactile without flattening the chemistry.

Choose a molecule you already know. See its fingerprint. Hear the pattern its bonds make.

Goals

  1. Let visitors pick a familiar molecule before they face a spectrum.
  2. Connect each colored dip to a named bond vibration on the structure.
  3. Use sound so frequency is felt, not only read as a number.

Experience structure

The prototype follows a three-beat loop for each of five molecules: Vanillin, Aspirin, Caffeine, Acetone, and Ethanol.

01

Meet the five

Home screen cards with everyday imagery. Each molecule has a Look inside path.

02

Eighteen-second intro

A short narrated beat: light meets the sample, the fingerprint layers, why it matters. Skip stays available.

03

Hear My Frequency

Tap a colored spectrum dip. The matching bond lights on the molecule and a tone plays for that vibration.

Process

The Chemistry Themed Animation app is a React and Vite front end with Tailwind. Interaction state covers molecule selection, intro playback, spectrum mode selection, and Web Audio tones. Figma Make scaffolded the visual system; I extended the component set for the exhibit flow.

Vanillin is the deepest path so far: full intro scenes, five interactive stretch modes, and a bond-highlight molecule view. The other four molecules share the same card and fingerprint model so the set can grow without a new interaction grammar.

I am also owning the backend path for the exhibit, but that work is still in planning. The current prototype runs client-side. Next is defining how molecule data, media, and any live exhibit services should sit behind the UI before implementation begins.

Timed intro keeps science brief, with an escape hatch to explore. Spectrum and structure stay linked by color while the bond is active.

Design decisions

Everyday molecules before abstract spectra

The decision

The first screen is a picker of five real-world compounds with photographic anchors, not a blank IR chart.

Why

Visitors need a mental hook. Vanilla and coffee invite curiosity. Wavenumber axes do not.

Color-linked dips, legend, and molecule

The decision

Each stretch mode (O-H, C-H, C=O, aromatic C=C, C-O) keeps one color across the spectrum point, the pill legend, and the highlighted bond.

Why

The mapping has to survive a glance. If the purple dip, purple label, and purple bond disagree, the lesson fails.

Sound as a second channel for frequency

The decision

Selecting a dip plays a sine tone scaled from the vibration class. Sequences can also play the molecule's fingerprint as a short motif.

Why

IR is about frequency. Hearing a difference between stretches makes the idea stick when the graph alone would not.

What is still open

  • Finish parity across Aspirin, Caffeine, Acetone, and Ethanol explore modes.
  • Plan and start the backend: data model, media delivery, and exhibit services the UI will call.
  • Fold educator workshop feedback into pacing, skip behavior, and label clarity.
  • Harden the prototype for exhibit hardware: kiosk scale, muted-first audio cues, and durable controls.

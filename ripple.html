Overview

TL;DR

The problem

Recommenders that only return more of what you already chose cannot produce anything new.

What I did

I designed and coded an interactive pond that narrows with preference, then pulls live Austin weather back in as outside randomness.

The outcome

A discursive piece in progress. Live link and final media still pending.

Context

Context and challenge

Ripple is a discursive design project based on James Bridle's Ways of Being, chapter 7, "Getting Random." The piece argues that closed preference loops erase chance encounters.

Viewers should feel the pond learn them, then feel what happens when the system can no longer invent variety from inside itself.

Real randomness has to come from outside the machine.

Goals

Goals

  1. Make preference collapse visible through interaction, not explanation alone.
  2. Use live weather as behavior, not only as decoration.
  3. Keep the opening instruction simple enough that clicking teaches the system.

Research

Research and insights

The research base is the reading itself. Two ideas structure the piece.

Recommenders reduce chance encounters

Systems optimized for preference remove the accidents that once brought unfamiliar things into view.

Selection without variation produces nothing new

If every next option is drawn only from past likes, the set can shrink until novelty disappears.

Outside input can reopen the set

Weather is not trained on the viewer's clicks. Connecting it back in is a way to argue for external randomness.

Decisions

Design decisions

Opening instruction: click the ripples you like

The decision

The piece starts with a plain instruction instead of a long explanation of recommenders.

Why

Preference has to be felt through action. The critique lands harder after the viewer has trained the pond.

Watercolor-inspired, noise-edged rings

The decision

Ripples use soft, irregular edges rather than hard geometric circles.

Why

The visual language needed to feel organic enough for a pond metaphor, while still reading as a system that can learn.

Weather controls behavior, not just color

The decision

Open-Meteo data changes how the pond behaves when variety returns, not only how it looks.

Why

If weather were only a tint, the argument about outside randomness would stay decorative.

Iterations

Iterations

  • Clicks not registering. Hit testing had to decide whether the ring or the word counted. I adjusted targets until preference felt reliable.
  • Counter climbing then dropping. I reframed the counter as engagement that never goes down, so the system did not punish earlier likes.
  • Gradient banding. Native radial gradients plus grain reduced banding without killing the watercolor feel.

Reflection

Reflection

What I learned

  • Discursive interaction works best when the critique arrives after the viewer has participated.
  • Outside data only supports the argument if it changes behavior.
  • Small interaction bugs (missed clicks, counters, banding) can break the metaphor faster than visual polish can save it.

What I would do next

  • Ship the live link and capture a clean hero video of the full arc.
  • Be explicit about the caveat: between weather fetches, the sequence is still deterministic.

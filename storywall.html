TL;DR

The problem

Interactive walls often fail not from lack of interest, but because the effort to understand them outweighs the reward. First-time visitors need curiosity without confidence, instruction, or commitment.

What I did

I owned the Rule Breaker proto-persona and its principles, helped shape the wall's interaction logic, and exclusively developed Anchored Memories as a complete narrative path. The team shipped the full TXI Final Project universe around those project planets.

The outcome

A curated coded experience: enter the universe, browse glowing project planets, and open cinematic capsules, with Anchored Memories as my end-to-end story contribution inside that system.

Context and challenge

StoryWall lives in a space dedicated to exploration and emerging technologies. Guests include tourists, hesitant first-timers, explorers, and people who push every boundary.

The design problem was not "show more projects." It was inviting low-pressure discovery that communicates TXI culture without demanding that visitors already know how to behave at an interactive wall.

The wall should welcome exploration without the fear of a wrong move, and people should want to come back curious, not leave embarrassed.

Goals

  1. Let the wall initiate first contact so visitors do not hunt for a Start button.
  2. Make value visible before effort, including for people who only watch.
  3. Keep the first interaction reversible, silent, and consequence-free.
  4. Reward curiosity with depth instead of punishing unconventional input.

Research and insights

We modeled visitors as behavioral mindsets under uncertainty, not demographic personas. Jobs to be done framed what people were trying to get done when they approached the wall.

I authored the Rule Breaker proto-persona. Teammates developed adjacent mindsets such as the Tourist, the Explorer, and the Overwhelmer. Together those shaped the merged experience principles.

Rule Breaker (mine)

Spontaneous, ignores instructions, asks "what happens if I don't follow the prompt?" Pulled in by discovery; pushed away by hard "not allowed" states with no reasoning.

Primary job

When encountering something unfamiliar, experiment with its constraints so meaning can form beyond the prescribed structure.

Attention budget

If the wall responds well, they stay longer. If it resists them, they disengage fast. Observation and hovering still count as participation.

States we designed for

The wall had to work across a full spectrum of presence, not only for confident touch.

01

Passing by

User does nothing. Wall still reacts subtly.

02

Peripheral noticing

User slows, looks briefly, no touch yet.

03

Hovering / watching

Stands nearby, observes others, unsure whether to engage.

04

Tentative interaction

Small gesture or partial touch from an unconfident input.

05

Confident exploration

User tests limits: clicks, moves, plays.

06

Graceful exit

User leaves. Wall resets without leftover shame states.

Design decisions

Curate a universe, not a menu

The decision

The final product organizes TXI work as orbiting planets around a branded sun, then opens each into a multi-screen cinematic capsule.

Why

Spatial browse keeps discovery playful and reversible. Depth lives inside planets instead of behind dense UI chrome.

The wall initiates first contact

The decision

Ambient starfield and presence-led cues come before commitment. Instruction is minimal; noticing is enough to begin.

Why

Value must appear before effort. If the wall waits for confidence, hesitant visitors never begin.

Curiosity is not punished

The decision

From the Rule Breaker principles: exploration should never cause harm, unintended use can count as participation, and if something cannot be done the wall should already answer why.

Why

Rule Breakers reinterpret the space instead of filling a form. Soft redirects keep them exploring instead of bouncing.

Own one path completely

The decision

Anchored Memories was taken from style sheets and storyboards through character art to a finished film, so at least one planet had a complete narrative standard.

Why

A curated universe needs proof that a project planet can hold a full story, not only a title and tagline.

Results and impact

  • 100+ Visitors engaged through TXI spatial experiences
  • 35% Engagement lift after onboarding flow work
  • 15 Usability tests informing spatial interaction patterns

Success criteria include dwell for non-interactors, fewer abandoned interactions, curiosity that increases time rather than ending it, and social engagement: people pointing, talking, and bringing others over.

Reflection

What I learned

  • Proto-personas work when they sound like nervous thought bubbles, not LinkedIn profiles.
  • A team product can still carry a clear individual authorship line when one path is owned end to end.
  • Public walls must design for passing by and graceful exit as carefully as for confident play.

What I would do next

  • Bring Anchored Memories media into the coded planet capsule at the same fidelity as the film.
  • Pressure-test universe browse and showcase timing with live visitors on the physical wall.
  • Measure attempt rate, unconventional retries, and social clustering around the wall.
